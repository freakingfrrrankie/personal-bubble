If a technological environment is understood only as something that should continuously perceive, anticipate and adapt to human needs, responsiveness risks becoming another model of seamless availability. The environment is expected to remain permanently accessible and accommodating.

Breathing Room proposes a different relationship. The environment senses a changing condition and is permitted to alter its own availability in response. Its contraction does not claim: I understand what you need. It might instead indicate: this relationship has reached a limit. The distinction matters because care is not identical to service. Care can involve proximity but also distance; attention but also restraint; responsiveness but also refusal. A boundary that can never resist or withdraw is not being negotiated. It is simply available to be crossed.

The curtain therefore does more than regulate the privacy of the bodies behind it. It gives the environment itself a material capacity to regulate relation. As bodies become increasingly present, the room becomes less available to them. This reverses a familiar direction of technological responsiveness. Rather than sensing users in order to provide greater access, convenience or personalisation, the system senses in order to negotiate whether its existing conditions of access should remain unchanged.

In this sense, responsiveness does not necessarily mean accommodation. The environment can register a body while also establishing a limit to what it makes available in return. This gives friction a reciprocal dimension. It marks not only the boundary of what a technological system is permitted to know about a body, but also the boundary of what an environment is expected to provide in return. The body does not have to become completely legible to the environment, and the environment does not have to remain completely available to the body.

The mechanism of Breathing Room makes this negotiation perceptible. What is sensed, what counts as presence, where the threshold lies and what response follows are designed decisions. Making those decisions visible does not make them neutral or automatically caring. It makes them available to encounter and to question. The proposed contraction of the membrane consequently remains ambiguous. It might be experienced as protection, intimacy or collective enclosure. It might equally produce pressure, restriction, exclusion or discomfort.

The room does not know the difference.
