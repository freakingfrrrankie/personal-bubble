The working prototype consequently tests the complete computational sequence, but not the proposed spatial consequence. The buzzer makes the rule environmentally perceptible through sound; the unrealised membrane would return it through the contraction and expansion of space. How this movement would be experienced remains untested.

What the prototype makes visible is a more fundamental transformation: a body becomes legible to the system by becoming something other than the body as lived.

Once presence has become a count, however, translation is no longer the only issue. The number has to be told what it means. And it has to be given permission to do something.

Was the beam interrupted?
There is no complete version of me inside the computer waiting to be reduced. What the system can know about me has already been decided by what its sensors are capable of noticing.

And from that tiny piece of me → sensor → count → computational rule → environmental response

Eventually, I want the environmental response to be the curtain walls move in and out. For now, it is only a buzzer. The membrane itself has not yet moved around a body. So I still do not know what that return will feel like. I only know that somewhere between my body and the room's response, I became 1. And what is 1 allowed to do?
