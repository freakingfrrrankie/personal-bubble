Embodiment names a mutual but uneven process, not a literal merging of body and tool. To embody a tool, space, or environment is to develop bodily habits through relation to it; in turn, the tool, space, or environment embodies the body by conditioning how it can perceive, move, act, and respond.

A relationship does not automatically constitute embodiment. Relationship describes the structure of interaction. Embodiment describes what that relationship does to bodily experience. Embodiment is what happens when a relationship becomes consequential for how a body perceives, moves, acts, or develops habits.
